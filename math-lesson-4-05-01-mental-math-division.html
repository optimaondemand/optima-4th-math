Essential Question
If 60 ÷ 3 = 20, what does 63 ÷ 3 equal — and how can a number bond make it easy?

63 = 60 + 3. So 63 ÷ 3 = (60 + 3) ÷ 3 = (60 ÷ 3) + (3 ÷ 3) = 20 + 1 = 21.

Split the dividend into pieces both divisible by 3. Divide each piece. Add the quotients. The number bond shows you the pieces.

Think About It
Look closely, then take a guess.
🍎

A teacher wants to put 72 apples into 3 equal groups. Atlas suggests: split 72 into 60 + 12. Why those pieces? Because BOTH divide by 3 cleanly. Show how the number bond gives the answer in two easy steps.

60 ÷ 3 = 20 (one piece). 12 ÷ 3 = 4 (other piece). Add: 20 + 4 = 24 apples per group.
72 ÷ 3 = (60 ÷ 3) + (12 ÷ 3) = 20 + 4 = 24.

📐 Three Mental Division Tools

1. Number bonds. Split the dividend into two pieces that BOTH divide evenly. Divide each piece. Add the quotients.
Example: 75 ÷ 5 = (50 ÷ 5) + (25 ÷ 5) = 10 + 5 = 15.

2. ÷10 patterns. If you know the basic division, the dividend's trailing zeros land on the quotient.
Example: 84 ÷ 7 = 12 → 840 ÷ 7 = 120 → 8,400 ÷ 7 = 1,200.

3. Place-value extension. The basic fact controls the family. 72 ÷ 6 = 12 means 72 tens ÷ 6 = 12 tens = 120 and 72 hundreds ÷ 6 = 12 hundreds = 1,200.

Worked Example: The Couch-Table-Chair Chain
Two divisions back to back.
Problem

A couch costs $360. It costs TWICE as much as a table. A table costs THREE TIMES as much as a chair. How much does the chair cost?

Watch the Math Crew solve it together:

⚖️ Couch → Table → Chair
Step 1. Table = couch ÷ 2 = $360 ÷ 2 = $180.
Step 2. Chair = table ÷ 3 = $180 ÷ 3 = (150 ÷ 3) + (30 ÷ 3) = 50 + 10 = $60.
Chair costs $60. Check: $60 × 3 = $180 (table). $180 × 2 = $360 (couch). ✓
