✅ 2, 4 and 12.
Partners are the same distance from the line. If 6 squares are shaded on one side, a finished symmetrical design has double: 12.
🔄 Picture a mirror.
Your reflection is as far behind the mirror as you are in front of it. A square folds 4 ways. Double means two of the same.

📐 What You Just Saw

To finish a symmetrical design, give every shaded square a partner. The partner is on the other side of the line, in the same row (or column), the same distance from the line.

Squares next to the line have partners next to the line. Squares far away have partners far away. That’s a flip, not a slide.

🗂️
Finding Partners
For a line that goes up and down
Shaded squareIts partner
Row 2, right next to the line on the leftRow 2, right next to the line on the right
Row 3, 3 squares left of the lineRow 3, 3 squares right of the line
Row 5, 5 squares left of the line (the edge)Row 5, 5 squares right of the line (the other edge)
Watch out: When the line goes across, partners are in the same column, the same number of rows above and below the line. Turn your head so the line is up and down if that helps.

Worked Example: The Library Window
Partner by partner
Problem

Half of the library window is done. Finish it so it is symmetrical about the dashed line.

Before

After

Row 2: two squares, 1 and 2 away from the line. Shade 1 and 2 away on the right.
Row 3: three squares, 1, 2 and 3 away. Shade 1, 2 and 3 away on the right.
Row 4: one square, right next to the line. Shade right next to the line.
Row 5: one square next to the line and one 4 away. Shade both partners.
Check: 8 squares on each side. Fold it in your head: every square lands on a partner. ✓
