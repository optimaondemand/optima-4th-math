Base-Ten Blocks

Units, rods, flats and cubes. Each piece is 10 times the one before it, and you can see it.

2-minute readGrades 1–5Kit: Base Ten Units, Rods, Flats

What it is

unit = 1rod = 10flat = 100cube = 1,000

Students can see that a ten is ten ones. That makes blocks the place to start place value.

Once students trust the blocks, move to Number Discs, where the size no longer gives the value away.

How to use the tool

  1. Drag a block from the tray onto its column.
  2. Ten or more in a column? Drag one up to the bigger column. Ten become one.
  3. Need to take away more than you have? Drag a block to the smaller column. It breaks into ten.
  4. Drag a block off the mat to take it away.

Try this with your class

46 + 38

  1. Build 46 and 38 on the mat.
  2. Ones: 6 + 8 = 14. Group 10 ones into 1 rod. 4 ones are left.
  3. Tens: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8 rods. The answer is 84.

Ask students to say the trade out loud: "10 ones make 1 ten."

Watch for

Calling a rod "1".Ask: how many units is that rod worth?
Counting every unit.Fine at first. Then ask: can you count by tens?
Staying on blocks too long.Blocks get bulky past 1,000. Move to discs for bigger numbers and decimals.
