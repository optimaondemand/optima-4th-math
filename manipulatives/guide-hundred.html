The Hundred Chart

A 10 by 10 grid of the numbers 1 to 100. Color it to see patterns.

2-minute readGrades K–5Kit: Hundred Number Board

1-minute video · captions on screen

What it is

Numbers 1 to 100 in rows of ten. Down one row is +10. Right one box is +1.

Because of the rows of ten, patterns jump out: skip-counting lines, columns that end in the same digit, and where two skip counts meet.

Where it shows up

K–2: counting, +10 and −10Grade 3: skip-counting, multiplication factsGrades 4–5: multiples, factors, primes

How to use the tool

  1. Tap a number, or drag across numbers, to color them.
  2. In Set up, choose a number to skip-count by. Those numbers light up.
  3. Pick a second number to see where both colors land.
  4. Ask: what pattern do you see?

Try this with your class

Color the multiples of 4. Then the multiples of 6.

  1. Where do both colors land? 12, 24, 36, 48…
  2. Those are common multiples. The smallest is 12.
  3. Ask: will 100 ever be in both? How can you tell without coloring?

Watch for

Row ends trip students up.39 + 1 is 40, at the end of the row, not the start of the next.
Guessing the pattern too soon.Ask students to check one more number before they say the rule.
A chart that starts at 0.Some charts run 0–99. Check which one students are using.
