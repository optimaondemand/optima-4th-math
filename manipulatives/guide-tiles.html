Color Tiles: Area & Perimeter

Small square tiles. Cover a shape to find its area. Walk its edge to find its perimeter.

2-minute readGrades 2–5Kit: Color Tiles, Grid Board

What it is

Area = squares insidePerimeter = edges around

Each tile is 1 square unit. Students mix up area and perimeter because both are "counting." Tiles show the difference: area counts squares, perimeter counts the outside edges.

How to use the tool

  1. Drag a tile onto the grid, or drag across the grid to lay a row.
  2. Drag a tile off the grid to pick it up.
  3. Count the squares for the area.
  4. Turn on the outline and count the edges for the perimeter.

Try this with your class

Use exactly 12 tiles. How many different rectangles can you make?

  1. Students find 1 × 12, 2 × 6 and 3 × 4. (Turned ones count as the same.)
  2. Find each perimeter: 26, 16, 14.
  3. Ask: same area, so why is the perimeter different? Which one is closest to a square?

Then flip it: make shapes that all have a perimeter of 12. Do they all have the same area?

Watch for

Counting corner squares for perimeter.Perimeter counts edges, not tiles. A corner tile has 2 outside edges.
Gaps and overlaps.Tiles must touch edge to edge, with no spaces.
Wrong units.Area is in square units (sq cm). Perimeter is in units (cm).
