The Decimal Grid

A 10-by-10 square that stands for 1 whole. Shade it to show tenths and hundredths.

2-minute readGrades 4–5Kit: Centimeter Grid Board

What it is

Whole grid = 11 column = 1 tenth = 0.11 small square = 1 hundredth = 0.01

The grid makes decimals look like what they are: parts of one whole. Students can see that 0.4 and 0.40 shade the same amount, and that each column is 10 small squares.

How to use the tool

  1. One whole grid is 1.
  2. Drag across squares to shade them.
  3. Count full columns first (tenths), then the extra squares (hundredths).
  4. Read the amount as a decimal and as a fraction: 0.58 = 58/100.

Try this with your class

Which is greater, 0.6 or 0.58?

  1. Shade 0.6 on one grid: 6 full columns.
  2. Shade 0.58 on another: 5 columns and 8 more squares.
  3. Compare. 0.6 is 60 squares. 0.58 is 58. 0.6 is greater.

Next step: 58 squares out of 100 is also 58%. The same grid shows all three.

Watch for

"Longer is bigger."Students think 0.58 > 0.6 because 58 > 6. Shade both and compare.
12 hundredths written as 0.012.Shade 12 squares. That's 1 column and 2 squares: 0.12.
Forgetting the whole.Ask: what does the full grid stand for? (1.)
