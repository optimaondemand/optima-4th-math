Set Squares & Ruler

Two plastic triangles, each with a square corner. Use them with a ruler to draw perpendicular and parallel lines.

2-minute readGrades 3–5Kit: Triangle Ruler Set

What it is

Both triangles have one square corner (90°). That corner does all the work.

One triangle has two 45° corners. The other has a 30° and a 60° corner. For grades 3–5, students only need the square corner.

Two moves

Perpendicular (a square corner)

  1. Put one short edge of the triangle on the line.
  2. Draw along the other short edge.

Parallel (never meet)

  1. Put a short edge of the triangle on the line. Push the ruler against the other short edge.
  2. Hold the ruler still. Slide the triangle along it.
  3. Draw along the same edge again. The two lines are parallel.

How to use the tool

  1. Pick Move. Drag a ruler or triangle to slide it. Drag its round handle to turn it.
  2. Pick Draw. Drag along an edge to draw a straight line.

Try this with your class

Draw a rectangle using only the set square and ruler.

  1. Draw one side.
  2. Draw two perpendicular lines from its ends.
  3. Draw the last side parallel to the first. Check every corner with the square corner.

Watch for

Using the long slanted edge.It doesn't make a square corner. Only the two short edges do.
The ruler slips.On paper, one hand holds the ruler flat while the other slides the triangle.
"They look parallel."Measure the gap at both ends. Parallel lines stay the same distance apart.
