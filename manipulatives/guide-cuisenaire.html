Cuisenaire Rods

Ten colored rods, 1 to 10 units long. No numbers printed on them. Students learn each color by its length.

2-minute readGrades K–2Kit: Cuisenaire Rods

What it is

Because there are no numbers on them, students reason with lengths: two reds are as long as one purple.

That makes rods good for number pairs, comparing, doubles and halves, and early fractions (a red is half of a purple).

How to start it with students

  1. Free play first. 10 minutes to build anything. Then talk about what they noticed.
  2. Build the staircase. Shortest to longest. How many whites make each rod?
  3. Make trains. Put rods end to end under a longer rod to match its length.

How to use the tool

  1. Drag a rod onto the mat.
  2. Tap a rod to turn it.
  3. Put rods under a long rod to match it.
  4. Drag a rod off the mat to put it away.

Try this with your class

Lay down a yellow rod. How many ways can you make a train just as long, using two rods?

  1. White + purple, red + light green, and the same pairs turned around.
  2. Write each one: 1 + 4 = 5, 2 + 3 = 5.
  3. Ask: how do you know you found them all?

Watch for

Counting rods, not length."I used 2 rods, so it's 2." Ask: how long is your train?
Ends not lined up.Trains must start at the same edge as the rod they match.
Skipping free play.Students who haven't played will play during the lesson instead.
