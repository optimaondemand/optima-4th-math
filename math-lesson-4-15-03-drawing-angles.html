📐 What You Just Saw

To draw an angle: draw one ray. Put the protractor’s center mark on the end point (the vertex) and line up the flat edge with the ray. Find the row that says 0 on your ray, count up to your number, and make a pencil mark. Then draw a ray from the vertex through the mark.

Check with an estimate: a 120° angle should look wider than a square corner.

Watch out: If you count from the wrong row, you’ll draw the partner angle. A mark at 160 on the wrong row makes a 20° angle, just like Wren’s. Estimate before you draw so you catch it.

Worked Example: A 70° Rail
When the first ray points left
Problem

Hazel’s first ray points left. She needs a 70° angle for the handrail.

Estimate. 70° is a little less than a square corner.
Place. Center mark on the end of the ray, flat edge along it.
Find the 0. The ray points left. The outer row says 0 on the left.
Mark. Count 10, 20… 70 on the outer row. Make a dot.
Draw and check. Ray from the vertex through the dot. A little less than a square corner. ✓
