Today's Mission
Take last lesson's rounding rule and stretch it to 6-digit season totals. Round to the nearest 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000 — and add one new place: 100,000. The rule is the same; the decider just moves one step left.
❓
Essential Question
Does the rounding rule change for bigger numbers?

Last lesson, you rounded 5-digit numbers like 23,847. Today the numbers are bigger — 6-digit season totals like 487,290.

Good news: the rule doesn't change. Find the decider digit. 5 or higher rounds up. 4 or lower rounds down. You just have a new place to aim for — nearest 100,000.

📐 What You Just Saw

The rule didn't change — only the size of the number did.

For ANY size number, the decider digit lives one place to the right of the place you're rounding to. 5 or higher rounds UP. 4 or lower rounds DOWN.

Rounding 487,290 to the nearest 100,000? The decider is the ten-thousands digit (8). Up it goes — to 500,000.

Watch for the carry: when the rounding-place digit is 9 and we round UP, it rolls to 0 and the next place ticks up. Sometimes that carry ripples across two or three places.

🔗 The Rule in One Table

Same number — 487,290. Five different rounding goals. Same rule every time.

Round to Decider digit Rounded answer
nearest 100 Tens: 9 → 5 or higher → round UP (with carry) 487,300
nearest 1,000 Hundreds: 2 → 4 or lower → round DOWN 487,000
nearest 10,000 Thousands: 7 → 5 or higher → round UP 490,000
nearest 100,000 Ten-thousands: 8 → 5 or higher → round UP 500,000

The rule: The DECIDER DIGIT is the one just to the right of the place you're rounding to. 5–9 means round up. 0–4 means round down. The rest of the digits to the right of the decider become zeros in the answer.

Worked Example: The Carry Case (599,500)
When rounding UP makes more than one digit change.
Problem

A multi-team festival reported exactly 599,500 total attendees. Round it to the nearest 1,000. Watch what happens — the answer is going to surprise you a little.

Watch the Math Crew work through it:

F
Felix — apply the rule
"Rounding to thousands. Decider is the hundreds digit — that's 5. Exactly 5. Halfway rounds UP. So thousands 9 should become… 10?"
A
Atlas — handle the overflow
"A place can only hold ONE digit. So the 10 thousands becomes 1 ten-thousand and 0 thousands. But ten-thousands was already 9 — so THAT becomes 10 too, which means 1 hundred-thousand. The 5 in hundred-thousands ticks up to 6."

599,500 → 600,000

H
Hazel — verify on the line
"Two neighbors are 599,000 and 600,000. Halfway is 599,500. The number IS at halfway, so we round up. The line agrees with the rule — 600,000 wins."
C
Cora — count what changed
"Look at how many digits changed: 599,500 → 600,000. The thousands, ten-thousands, AND hundred-thousands all moved. The carry rippled all the way through. Same rule — just a bigger chain reaction."
⚠️

About the carry chain: Most rounds change only ONE place to the left of the rounding place (or none at all if rounding down). But when the digit at the rounding place is 9 and we round UP, it spills over. That spill can keep going if the next place is ALSO 9. The rule never changes — it's just place value doing its job.

⚖️
Round Up vs Round Down — Same Decider Place
Two numbers, same rounding goal, two different directions.

Both numbers below are being rounded to the nearest 10,000. The decider digit is the thousands digit in each. Watch what's different.

NUMBER A — 384,200
Decider digit (thousands): 4. Four is 4 or lower → round DOWN. 380,000. The ten-thousands stays 8; everything to its right becomes 0.
NUMBER B — 387,200
Decider digit (thousands): 7. Seven is 5 or higher → round UP. 390,000. The ten-thousands ticks from 8 to 9; everything to its right becomes 0.
🧐

Same decider place. Same rounding goal. Just 3 thousand more separating the two numbers — and that's enough to flip the answer by 10,000. Rounding magnifies small differences at the decider place.

✏️ What's the SMALLEST change you could make to 384,200 that would change its rounded answer (to nearest 10,000) from 380,000 to 390,000? Use place value to defend your answer.
