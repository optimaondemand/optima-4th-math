✅ 3, 14 and 2.
Break one of the 4 ones into 10 tenths: 3 ones and 15 tenths. Break one of the 3 tenths into 10 hundredths: 2 tenths and 14 hundredths. You’ll do both kinds of breaks today.
🔄 Same amount, smaller pieces.
When you break 1 one, the ones go down by 1 and the tenths go up by 10. When you break 1 tenth, the tenths go down by 1 and the hundredths go up by 10.

📐 What You Just Saw

Line up the decimal points. If one number has fewer places, fill in zeros: 5.6 becomes 5.60.

Start with the hundredths. If the top digit is too small, break a tenth into 10 hundredths. Then do the tenths. If that top digit is too small, break a one into 10 tenths. Then do the ones.

Check by counting up on a number line, or by adding your answer to the smaller number.

Worked Example: Mia’s Second Jump
Back to the long jump from the Warm-Up
Problem

On her second try, Mia jumped 3.9 m. Her first jump was 3.42 m. How much farther was her second jump?

810
3.90
−3.42
0.48
onestenthshundredths
Step 1. Fill in a zero: 3.9 = 3.90.
Step 2. Hundredths: 0 − 2? Not enough. Break a tenth: 8 tenths and 10 hundredths. 10 − 2 = 8.
Step 3. Tenths: 8 − 4 = 4. Ones: 3 − 3 = 0. Mia’s second jump was 0.48 m farther.
Check by counting up. 3.42 → 3.50 is 0.08. 3.50 → 3.90 is 0.40. 0.08 + 0.40 = 0.48. ✓
