Today's Mission
You know the algorithm. Today you learn when NOT to use it. When a number is close to a friendly one (200, 1,000, 5,000…), a quick mental swap can be faster than stacking and carrying.
❓
Essential Question
When does the standard algorithm win, and when does a mental strategy win?

The standard algorithm always works. But it is not always the fastest. When a number is close to a friendly number — 200, 1,000, 5,000 — there is usually a quicker mental move.

Smart math is not just knowing one way. It is looking at the numbers first, then choosing.

📐 What You Just Saw

Three strategies for adding multi-digit numbers.

1. Make a Friendly Number. Bump one addend up or down to a clean 100, 1,000, or 5,000. Do the easy addition. At the end, take back what you added (or add back what you took away). Best when one number is within 5 of a friendly value.

2. Decompose by Place. Split each number into thousands, hundreds, tens, ones. Add place by place. Best when the place values are already clean (lots of zeros).

3. Standard Algorithm. Stack, add, carry. Always works. Use when the other two don't fit.

Cora's rule: estimate FIRST. Then check that your strategy's answer is close to your estimate. If they're far apart, you lost track somewhere.

Watch a Friendly Bump Live: 2,997 + 4,128
Step through Felix's compensation move.

Felix says 2,997 is "screaming for a bump." Walk through his move and watch what happens to the answer.

Original: 2,997 + 4,128 = ?
Step 1. Bump 2,997 up to 3,000 (added +3).
Step 2. Easy add: 3,000 + 4,128 = 7,128.
Step 3. Take back the 3 (because we added it): 7,128 − 3 = 7,125.
Final answer: 7,125. Check with estimate: 3,000 + 4,000 = 7,000. ✓ Close.
