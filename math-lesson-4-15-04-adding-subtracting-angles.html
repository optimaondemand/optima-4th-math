📐 What You Just Saw

When angles sit side by side and share a vertex, their measures add up to the whole angle: 50° + 35° = 85°.

To find a missing part, subtract the part you know from the whole: 120° − 45° = 75°. Use landmarks when the whole isn’t labeled: a right angle is 90°, and a straight line is 180°.

Which Equation?
Read the picture, then pick the operation

Both parts: 50° and 35°The whole50 + 35 = 85
The whole (120°) and one part (45°)The other part120 − 45 = 75
It’s a right angle, one part is 28°The other part90 − 28 = 62
It’s a straight line, one part is 65°The other part180 − 65 = 115
Watch out: A part can never be bigger than the whole. If you get 165° for a part of a 120° angle, you added when you should have subtracted. Check by adding your parts: they must make the whole.

Worked Example: Three Angles on the Deck
More than two parts
Problem

Two rails lean up from the flat deck, making three angles along a straight line. Two of them are 40° and 75°. What is the third angle?

Find the whole. The deck is a straight line, so all three parts make 180°.
Add the parts you know. 40 + 75 = 115.
Subtract. 180 − 115 = 65.
Answer. The third angle is 65°.
Check. 40 + 75 + 65 = 180 ✓
