📐 What You Just Saw

Sometimes you run out of digits before you run out of leftovers. In 4.5 ÷ 2, after sharing the tenths, 1 tenth is still left. Write a 0 in the hundredths place: 4.5 = 4.50. Now trade the tenth for 10 hundredths and keep sharing.

10 hundredths ÷ 2 = 5 hundredths. 4.5 ÷ 2 = 2.25. Check: 2 × 2.25 = 4.50 ✓

Watch out: The 0 goes at the end, after the last digit. 4.5 = 4.50, but 4.5 is not 4.05. A 0 squeezed in between changes the amount: 4.05 is only 4 and 5 hundredths, like $4.05.

Worked Example: Three Liters for Four
Even a whole number can have zeros added
Problem

There are 3 L of iced tea left. Mia shares it equally among 4 pitchers. How much is in each pitcher?

0.7543.00−030−2820−200
Estimate. 3 L for 4 pitchers. Each gets less than 1 L.
Write zeros. 3 = 3.00. Now there’s room for tenths and hundredths.
Ones. 3 ÷ 4 = 0. Write 0 on top. 3 ones left.
Tenths. Trade: 30 tenths. 30 ÷ 4 = 7. 4 × 7 = 28. 2 tenths left.
Hundredths. Trade: 20 hundredths. 20 ÷ 4 = 5. 0 left.
Answer. 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 L. Check: 4 × 0.75 = 3.00 ✓
