Readiness Check: One Step at a Time
Every two-step story starts with a one-step piece.
~3 min

Find each part. Type a whole number.

✅ Both parts are right.
34 of 24: 24 ÷ 4 = 6 in each group, and 3 groups make 18. 56 of 30: 30 ÷ 6 = 5 in each group, and 5 groups make 25. Today you'll use a part like this as the first step of a longer story.
🔄 Let's rebuild it.
The denominator tells you how many equal groups to split the whole into. The numerator tells you how many groups to take. For 34 of 24: split 24 into 4 groups (6 each), then take 3 of them. Try the second one the same way: split 30 into 6 groups first.

The story says 14. How many equal groups make fourths?
Step 3 · How many cups are left in the cooler?
✅ 12 cups left.
Morning: 14 of 48 is 12 cups, so 36 cups were left. Lunch: 23 of those 36 is 24 cups. 36 − 24 = 12. The middle number, 36, was the key. You couldn't find the lunch part until you knew it.
🔄 Let's look at the middle number.
Build both steps on the bar first. "23 of the rest" means 23 of what's left after the morning, not 23 of all 48 cups. Find what's left, split that into 3 equal groups, and pour 2 of them.

Builder 2: Tug-of-War Points
As many as
Field Day tug-of-war. The Red team scored 24 points. The Blue team scored 34 as many points as the Red team. How many points did the two teams score altogether?
Step 1 · Split the Red bar into equal units
Red team · 24 points
Step 2 · Build the Blue bar out of Red-sized units
Blue team
Split the Red bar to start.
The story says 34 as many. What split makes fourths?
Step 3 · How many points altogether?
✅ 42 points altogether.
One Red unit is 24 ÷ 4 = 6 points. Blue is 3 of those units: 18 points. Together the bars hold 7 units, and 7 × 6 = 42. Did you spot the bonus? Red's bar is one unit longer, so Red won by 6 points.
🔄 Build Blue from Red's units.
Finish both bars first. "34 as many as Red" means split Red's 24 points into 4 equal units, then give Blue 3 of those same units. Count every unit in both bars, then multiply by what one unit is worth.
📐 What You Just Saw

A two-step story hides a middle number. Find it first, then use it.

"Of the rest" means the second fraction is a fraction of what's left, so draw a new bar for what's left.

"As many as" means build a second bar out of the first bar's units.

Worked Example: Back to the Track
Sam and Jo's laps from the Warm-Up
Problem

One lap is 34 mile. Sam ran 7 laps. Jo ran 4 laps. How much farther did Sam run than Jo?

Way 1: find both, then subtract.
Sam: 7 × 34 = 214 = 5 14 miles.
Jo: 4 × 34 = 124 = 3 miles.
5 14 − 3 = 2 14 miles.
Way 2: find the extra laps first.
Sam ran 7 − 4 = 3 more laps.
3 × 34 = 94 = 2 14 miles.
Check: 2 14 is between 2 and 3. How close was your Warm-Up prediction?

1A pack has 36 stickers. Maya used 59 of them on a poster. How many stickers are left in the pack?

stickers
✅ 16 stickers.
59 of 36: 36 ÷ 9 = 4 in each unit, and 5 units make 20 stickers used. 36 − 20 = 16 left. (Or: 4 units are left, and 4 × 4 = 16.)
🔄 Two steps.
First find the stickers Maya used: split 36 into 9 equal units and take 5. Then the question asks what's left, so take that amount away from 36.
✏️ My bar model in words

2A school garden has 40 plants. 38 of them are tomato plants. 25 of the rest are pepper plants. How many pepper plants are in the garden?

pepper plants
✅ 10 pepper plants.
Tomatoes: 38 of 40 = 15. The rest: 40 − 15 = 25 plants. Peppers: 25 of 25 = 10. The middle number was 25.
🔄 Watch for "of the rest."
The peppers are 25 of the plants that are not tomatoes. Find the tomatoes first, take them away from 40, and then find 25 of what's left.
