Readiness Check: Read the Scale
Only every other line has a number.
~3 min

Each small tick is 5 more than the one below it. What number does each arrow point to?

Arrow A:Arrow B:
✅ 65 and 85.
Arrow A sits halfway between 60 and 70, so it points to 65. Arrow B sits halfway between 80 and 90, so it points to 85. A line graph’s scale works the same way.
🔄 Count by fives.
The labels go up by 10, but there is a tick halfway between each pair. That middle tick is 5 more than the label below it. Start at the label under the arrow and count up by 5.

Graph Reader: The Rooftop Weather Station
Three jobs on one graph
The weather station. It checked the temperature every two hours. Each grid line is worth 5 degrees.
Job 1 · Read two points

Click a time to drop a guide line. Follow it across to the scale.

10 AM:°F6 PM:°F
✅ 75°F and 60°F.
The 10 AM point sits halfway between 70 and 80, so it’s 75. The 6 PM point sits right on the 60 line. Down to the time, across to the scale.
🔄 Two straight moves.
Click the time to drop a guide line.
Job 2 · Tag each piece of the line

Click a piece of the line to tag it. Click again to change the tag.

risingfallingflat
✅ Every piece is tagged right.
The temperature rose from 6 AM to 2 PM, then fell until 6 PM. No piece is flat. Reading left to right, uphill means warmer and downhill means cooler.
🔄 Read left to right.
Tag all 6 pieces first.
Job 3 · Compare the changes

Each bar shows how far the line moved in two hours. Compare the bars, then find the size of each change.

Biggest rise:degreesBiggest drop:degrees
✅ A 15-degree rise and a 20-degree drop.
The biggest rise was 8 AM to 10 AM: 75 − 60 = 15. The biggest drop was 4 PM to 6 PM: 80 − 60 = 20. Those are the two steepest pieces of the line. Steeper means a bigger change.
🔄 Find the steepest pieces.
Show the change bars and compare them.
📐 What You Just Saw

To read a point: go down to the time, then across to the scale.

To read the trend: go left to right. Uphill is an increase. Downhill is a decrease. Level is no change.

To compare changes: the steeper the piece, the bigger the change. Subtract the two readings to find its size.

Worked Example: The Afternoon Cool-Down
Back to the weather station from the Warm-Up
Problem

When was it warmest on the roof? How many degrees did the temperature drop from then until 6 PM?

Step 1. Find the highest point. It’s at 2 PM.
Step 2. Read it. Halfway between 80 and 90: 85°F.
Step 3. Read 6 PM. Right on the line: 60°F.
Step 4. Subtract. 85 − 60 = 25 degrees.
Check. Piece by piece: 2 PM to 4 PM dropped 5, then 4 PM to 6 PM dropped 20. 5 + 20 = 25. ✓
F
Felix
"I didn’t have to add the pieces. Reading the two ends and subtracting was faster."
C
Cora
"The answer is 25 degrees, not 25°F. It’s how much it changed, not how warm it was."
