This Unit at a Glance

Your student learns to change a measurement into a different unit: millimeters to meters, feet to inches, quarts to gallons, minutes to hours. The amount never changes. Only the unit and the number change, the way 100 pennies and 1 dollar are the same money.

The big idea

Going to a smaller unit? Multiply. You need more of the small pieces.
Going to a bigger unit? Divide. You need fewer of the big pieces.

The story

This unit is Level 10: Metric Market. A grumpy cloud called the Smudge has mixed up every measure in town. Each lesson fixes one problem, and the unit ends with a "Brick Golem" made of mixed-up units.

How We Teach It

Your student first builds each unit out of smaller pieces on the screen, like filling 1 centimeter with 10 millimeter beads. Then they draw it, and only then write the numbers. The picture we come back to all unit is a staircase:

⬇ Down to a smaller unit: multiply
⬆ Up to a bigger unit: divide
Different units? Make them match first, then add or subtract.

Answers with two units. Your student learns to write amounts like 5 L 450 mL or 15 gal 2 qt. The second part is the part that doesn't fill a whole big unit, and it's part of the answer, not a remainder to drop.

Mix-Ups to Watch For

  • Multiplying when they should divide. Ask: "Is the new unit bigger or smaller? So should the number get bigger or smaller?"
  • Using 100 for every metric change. Liters to milliliters is 1,000, not 100.
  • Treating time like money. 1 hour is 60 minutes, so 1 h 30 min is 90 minutes, not 130.
  • Dropping the leftover part. 62 quarts is 15 gallons and 2 quarts.
  • An answer without a unit. "700" isn't finished. "700 m" is.
🏠

Try It at Home

  1. Kitchen pour. With a measuring cup and an empty quart or half-gallon jug, have your student guess how many cups fill it, then pour to check. (4 cups fill a quart; 8 fill a half gallon.)
  2. Label hunt. Find a 2-liter bottle, a cereal box and a can. Read the units together. How many milliliters is the 2-liter bottle? Which labels show grams and ounces?
  3. Height chart. Measure everyone in feet and inches, then change each height to just inches.
  4. Movie math. A movie runs 1 h 52 min. How many minutes is that? If it starts at 6:15, when does it end?
  5. Ruler fractions. Measure a pencil or spoon to the nearest quarter inch. What fraction of a foot is it?

The best question to ask at home: "How do you know your answer makes sense?"
